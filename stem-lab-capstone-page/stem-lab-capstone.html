STEM Lab Redesign

Room 1133 · 1,255 SF

A layout redesign for my high school's STEM lab. I started from the room's architectural floor plan, mapped where every station actually lived, and worked the new layout out as successive red-line revisions — then drew the proposal directly over footage of the room to check it in three dimensions.

Rev 0

Existing Conditions

The base sheet is the lab's architectural floor plan. Before changing anything I labeled what was really in the room and where: the teaching desk at the front wall, the soldering station and computers along the window wall, TVs, storage shelves, the tool cabinet in the back corner, and the printers on the interior wall. The benches sat in two large clusters in the middle of the floor.

Rev 1

First Red-Line Pass

The first pass marked up the existing plan in red: break up the bench clusters, recover wall space at the front for storage, and track where every displaced piece of furniture would end up.

  • R1.1Center bench clusters split up and spread across the floor.
  • R1.2Front wall beside the teaching desk recast as a storage zone.
  • R1.3Locking cabinet flagged by the side door.
  • R1.4Arrows track furniture relocated off the window wall and back corner.
Rev 2

Refined Layout

Rev 2 settles the floor into rows of benches aligned across the room, opening a clear aisle down the center. The back corner by the tool cabinet gets a raised platform with storage underneath, so floor area isn't spent on storage alone.

  • R2.1Benches redrawn as rows, with a center aisle between the two halves.
  • R2.2Back corner raised on a lift for storage underneath.
  • R2.3Doors added to the front-wall storage beside the teaching desk.
Perspective

Drawn Into the Room

A plan view only shows so much, so I sketched the proposal directly over footage of the lab: benches in rows facing the front wall, a shelving unit and whiteboard with a door on the teaching wall, and the ceiling projector moved forward. The linework was then lifted off the photo into clean perspective sketches.

Additional Work

History of Gaming Panel

The lab's laser-cut History of Gaming board is a 9th-grade CAD class project built from interlocking gear-driven panels. Using the same overlay method, I sketched a replacement for the maze panel: a Mario Kart panel with two karts driven along their tracks by a gear train.
